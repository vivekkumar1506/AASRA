Knowledge Hub • Clear Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Common queries prospective parents have regarding regulations, timelines, eligibility, and legal rights.

How long does it typically take to adopt a child in India?

+
The timeline varies by child age category. For infants (0–2 years), the domestic queue on CARINGS averages 2.5 to 3.5 years due to high applicant volume. For young children (4–8 years), older children (8+ years), sibling groups, or children with medical needs, matching takes only 6 to 18 months, with priority placement options available.

Can a single individual legally adopt a child?

+
Yes! Under Indian law (CARA Regulations Section 5), a single female can adopt a child of any gender (boy or girl). A single male is eligible to adopt only a male child. Single prospective adoptive parents must be at least 25 years old.

What happens during the Home Study Report (HSR)?

+
A qualified social worker from your assigned local Specialized Adoption Agency visits your residence. The visit is not a check for luxury or opulence; it evaluates your living environment's safety, family hygiene, stability, and emotional alignment with raising an adopted child. The completed HSR is valid for 3 years.

How is the child legally declared ours?

+
Following the Pre-Adoption Foster Care agreement, the adoption agency files a petition before the District Magistrate (DM) under the Juvenile Justice Act. The DM passes a final, irrevocable Adoption Order, following which the local municipal registrar issues a fresh Birth Certificate with the adoptive parents registered as mother and father.
